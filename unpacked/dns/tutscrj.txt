0 In Shattered Galaxy, you will operate in one of two modes.
1 This mode is hero mode.  It is used for interaction with other players, buying and designing units, and assorted organizational purposes.
2 In this tutorial, I will demonstrate the user interface for hero mode.
3 The second mode is unit mode.  Unit mode is used during combat.
4 Clicking on your hero shows your hero's status.
5 On the left side are your hero's stats for one of the four divisions of units.
6 These buttons allow you to change the division shown.
7 Experience level for this division.  Maximum is 99.
8 Rank in that division.  An alphabetic translation of level.
9 The combined level of the units you own that belong to this division.
10 Experience your hero has earned in this division.  Heros earn experience when their units gain levels.
11 How much experience must be earned before reaching the next level in this division.
12 Prestige will provide a system of ranking between players.  Currently, an implementation similar to the USCF ranking system is planned.
13 Battles won/Battles lost.  Battles against aliens count.
14 Medals, honors, etc.  Currently not implemented.
15 Testing characters will be cleared of possessions and levels before commercial service begins.  We intend to permanently acknowledge testers through Accomplishments.
16 Political Title.
17 The division in which the hero has the most experience.
18 The hero's rank in his specialty.
19 Hero attributes.
20 The number of attribute points available for spending.  One attribute point is earned every time a hero gains a level.
21 Attribute points are spent using the appropriate arrow buttons.
22 Money and resources.  Wealth.  
23 We have plans for a dynamic economy, with different nations and planets and provinces having different reserves for each of the four resources.
24 Such a system will place strategic importance on warfare, as nations struggle for resources they lack.
25 This aspect of the game will be developed early on in testing.
26 The Resource Market allows players to convert money into resources and vice versa.  Exchange rates are dynamic and will later vary from nation to nation.
27 Unit Deployment.  The units in the section on top are deployed for battle.  The units on the bottom will not fight.
28 Single-clicking on a unit selects it.
29 Double-clicking on a unit switches it from active to reserve duty and vice versa.
30 Each deployment position corresponds to a hot key as shown.
31 Pressing a hot key during battle selects that unit.  Pressing a hot key twice centers the action on that unit.  Backspace selects all units.
32 Information on the active unit is shown in the bottom portion of the screen.
33 Except for Passive Energy, these should be self-explanatory.
34 Each unit has limited energy reserves with which to fight a battle.  If a unit runs out of energy, it cannot move or attack.
35 Energy usage is divided into two types: Passive and Active energy.
36 Active energy is energy spent by firing weapons or using special abilities.  You have control over active energy spent, since you control your unit's actions.
37 Passive energy is used by your unit's computer, engine, armor, sensors, and miscellaneous equipment.  It is constant throughout battle.
38 This gauge provides an estimate of your unit's energy reserves.
39 This dark region shows what portion of your unit's total energy will be spent as passive energy in the time frame shown.
40 Planet Map.  Provinces controlled by each nation are shown.
41 Small hero icons show where other users are located.  
42 If your hero moves into a region not owned by his own nation (or an allied nation), you will enter unit mode and fight a battle for the province.
43 Provinces not owned by any nation are swarming with alien lifeforms that must be defeated to conquer the province.
44 The alien lifeforms spawn from underground caverns whose air is too corrosive for permanent control by humans.
45 Unoccupied provinces will eventually be reclaimed by the aliens.
46 Shattered Galaxy has three communication modes.  For each, you communicate by pressing <Enter> and then the text you wish to transmit.
47 The first mode is speech.  Anything spoken appears above your hero and all around you will hear.
48 The second mode is through a com channel.  Enter the name of the person you wish to transmit to.  Only they will receive your transmission.
49 The third mode is an encoded transmission.  This mode is only used in the battlefield.  Only your allies will be able to decode your transmission.

68 We will now visit the lab to upgrade the Footman's weaponry.
69 The 'New' option allows units to be designed completely from scratch.  We want to upgrade Roland.
70 First, we must choose which unit to upgrade.  
71 The selected unit's stats are shown in the equipment summary window.  In this case, the equipment is Roland.
72 Note that your hero is one of the options.
73 By 'upgrading' your hero, you can buy equipment that can later be used to outfit your units without returning to the lab.
74 Double-clicking on a chassis begins the upgrading process.
75 The menu at the top shows the various equipment types available.
76 Below this menu are items available to you of that type.
77 How many items you will see will depend upon your unit's level and your education.
78 To the bottom left is a schematic of your unit, shown with the equipment it currently uses.
79 When an item is selected, its attributes appear in the equipment summary window.
80 Below the equipment summary window is the design summary window.
81 Each chassis can hold only a certain amount of weight.  The amount of weight your unit can hold increases as your Mechanical Aptitude increases.
82 The space restriction is currently not implemented.
83 Your unit's computer governs the maximum complexity of items your unit can use.
84 Engine strain measures how overburdened your unit's engine is.  The higher the engine strain, the more passive energy your unit will consume in battle.
85 When an item is selected, the design summary window shows the change to your unit that using that item will have.
86 For example, upgrading Roland's engine to the Light Javelin increases how much weight Roland is carrying, but decreases his engine strain.
87 Note that even though the Light Javelin uses more energy than Roland's Tinker Gizmo, passive energy requirements decrease because of the lower engine strain.
88 Double-clicking on an item in the lab equips it, automatically unequipping the old item.
89 The old item can either be sold or put into your hero's inventory for later use.
90 Let's upgrade Roland's weapon.
91 For weapons, several standard prefixes are used: V = Versatile (can attack both air and ground units); X = eXtra range; C = Cooling system (shorter cooldown time); H = Heavy (more damage)
92 Let's give Roland a powerful Gatling Rifle.
93 Now Roland's items are too complex for his computer to support.  We must upgrade his computer.
94 The Orion Mark III is the smallest computer that will be capable of handling Roland's new equipment.
95 Now the complexity requirement has been satisfied.  But Roland's chassis cannot hold so much weight.  If my hero's Mech Apt were high enough, I would not have this problem.
96 Selling Roland's armor makes his design acceptable.
97 The resource icons show how much these changes will cost.  If I had insufficient resources available, the icon would appear red to indicate an error.
98 The 'Done' button completes the upgrade of Roland.  Our tour of the unit design interface is complete.
